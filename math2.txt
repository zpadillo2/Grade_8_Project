An algebraic equation is a mathematical statement asserting that two algebraic expressions are equal, joined by an equal sign (
). Unlike an expression, which is a phrase, an equation is a complete sentence representing a balanced relationship between two 
quantities. It generally consists of variables (letters representing unknown values), 
coefficients (numbers multiplying variables), and constants (fixed numbers). The goal 
when working with these equations is to "solve" them, which means finding the specific 
value of the variable that makes the equation true. To maintain this balance, any operation 
applied—addition, subtraction, multiplication, or division—must be performed on both sides 
of the equation.
